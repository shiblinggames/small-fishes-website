01 How a round plays
  1. Decide in secret. Every player has two indicators, Hunt and Fish. Each round you pick one and put it face down. Nobody knows who is coming for the treasure until the reveal.
  2. Fishers build their crew. If you went fishing, you take two cards from the market. Fish come in three tiers of strength. Tier 1 fish are free or cheap; the big ones cost coins. Every fish has its own ability, and the higher its speed, the earlier it acts in a fight.
  3. Hunters battle for the treasure. Everyone who chose Hunt plays fish from their hand. Abilities combo and cancel each other out, and the highest total power wins the treasure card and becomes the captain. Treasure cards are worth 1 to 5, and 5 is worth the most.
  4. Encounters shake things up. Some treasure cards are encounters instead. Black Market makes fishing cheaper, Stormy Seas weakens every fish in the next hunt, Set Sail forces a discard and redraw, and Whaelstrom ignores every ability. Flip the next card to see what awaits.

The tension is the choice. Fishing makes you stronger for later, but the hunters may walk off with a five-point treasure while you are stocking up. Hunting with a weak crew wastes a turn. Reading the table is the whole game.

Whoever has the most treasure at the end wins. Rank breaks ties.

03 Questions before you buy
How many people can play, and how long does a game take?

Two to six players. A game runs 30 to 60 minutes depending on the table. You can shorten it by playing with fewer treasure cards.

What age is it for?

Ages 8 and up. The rules fit on a card; the bluffing is what grows with the players.

Where can I buy it?

From the Shibling Shop for $29.99. Holographic cards, a captain's emblem and a playmat are sold separately.

Is there a video that teaches the rules?

Yes. The how-to-play page has the video and a FAQ on effects, ties and card timing.

Can I play it online?

Not the card game itself, but every shop order includes pack codes for Small Fishes Online, a free browser game set in the same world.
